Insight on CO₂ emission

CO₂ emissions are one of the main causes of climate change, mainly due to human activities such as burning fossil fuels and deforestation.
Tracking these emissions, particularly on a per capita basis, helps to identify the countries that contribute most to global pollution.
This site visualises CO₂ emissions data, highlighting key trends and top emitters to raise awareness of environmental impacts and the need for sustainable solutions.

Explanation

The chart was created based on data from the file co-emission-per-capita.csv.

The original dataset included a mixture of data types:

  • countries
  • continents
  • economic categories (such as "high income" and "low income")
To focus solely on individual countries for this analysis, we filtered the dataset to exclude rows that didn't correspond to specific nations. Using Python, we cleaned and processed the data to extract only the relevant rows for countries. We then ranked these countries based on their CO₂ emissions per capita and selected the top 10 emitters for 2022.

Finally, the cleaned and sorted data was imported into D3.js, where it was visualized as a bar chart to allow easy comparison of emissions across these top-polluting countries.

Explanation

Plots are based on Per capita CO₂ emissions dataset (countries_data.csv).

To combine it with continents and population additionally we used another dataset World Population Dataset (world_population.csv) with few adjustments. The name of some countries were written in different way so we needed to change file mannually:

  • "Republic of the Congo" was changed to "Congo"
  • "DR Congo" -> "Democratic Republic of Congo"
  • "Timor-Leste" -> "East Timor"
  • "Macau" -> "Macao"
From "United Stated" capital "Washington, D.C." we removed ',' as to avoid conflicts when reading data from .csv file in fact separed by ','. Some countries names also needed to be renamed in origin dataset (countries_data.csv, after adding continent names it was replaced with countries_data_with_continents.csv)
  • "Micronesia (country)" was renamed to "Micronesia"
  • "Sint Maarten (Dutch part)" -> "Sint Maarten"
  • "Czechia" -> "Czech Republic"
All of above changes were made based on matching capital of each country. Whats more we excluded countries Saint Helena (island), Kosovo (declared independence in 2008), Cote d'Ivoire, Bonaire Sint Eustatius and Saba due to lack of population data.
We checked data validity (missing values and duplicates), filtered out zero-value emissions, standardized country names in the emissions dataset to match those in the population data (for meging consistency). Transformed population data from a wide to a long format, combined emissions and population data on country and year fields For each continent and year: Identified the top 5 emitting countries by CO₂ emissions per capita, calculated weighted average emissions for "Others" (non-top-5) and total continent-wide emissions. Processed and aggregated data by year was splitted into files continent_emissions_2000.csv, continent_emissions_2010.csv... etc. Those files are used for data visualizations.

Comment

Large countries are aware of negative influence of CO2 emissions. Take a look at United Stated - it is top first emitter in 2000 with 21,28 CO2 per capita and biggest population of all analysed countries - 282.40M. When analysing data year by year we see that the population increases (in deed of all countries, we can see maximums per each continent). From 2010 to now on, United Stated, with population of 311.18M is not top emitter anymore in North America.

Usually we can see that countries with top emissions are not marked with color as the largest populations among other emitters. We can conclude the trend of countries that they started to be more and more aware of the CO2 emissions impact on the environment - some rules have been implemented!

Based on two above plots it can be observed that Asia (mostly leading in total emissions), with larger population (look at the population values at total bar, and its color comparing to other continents), contribute to global emissions primarily through a larger cumulative effect rather than high individual emission rates (the width of the total bar!)

The "Others" section and its averaged emission shows that many countries outside the top five emitters in each continent, with large number of its total population, generally have no big influence and have lower emissions per capita.
