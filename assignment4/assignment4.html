Climate Change in New York

Climate change, driven by CO₂ emissions from fossil fuels and deforestation, is a critical global challenge. This website highlights global emission patterns and their impacts. In New York, rising emissions have led to milder winters and hotter summers, with 2023 showing a clear temperature spike since 1978, reflecting the local effects of a global crisis.

This website provides a compelling visualization of temperature changes in New York state from 1978 to 2023, offering insights into the impact of climate
change over the decades. The integration of time-series data and detailed charts effectively highlights trends such as increasing summer heat and milder winters.

The Heat is Rising

This plot compares monthly minimum, average and maximum temperatures in New York between 1978 and 2023. There is an increase across all months, especially the warmer summer peaks what highlights the effects of rising emissions and climate change over decades. Winters are less cold, and summers are noticeably hotter, with 2023 showing a clear temperature spike compared to 1978.

Monthly Temperature Trends in New York
