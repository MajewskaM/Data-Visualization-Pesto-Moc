Climate Change

Climate change is one of the most pressing challenges of our time, driven largely by CO₂ emissions from human activities.
These emissions primarily stem from the burning of fossil fuels and land-use changes, such as deforestation and agriculture.
This website explores global emission patterns, using visualizations to highlight the contributions of different continents and their most significant polluting countries.

What you should know

Hover here In 2022, more than 70% of all global greenhouse gas emissions came from fossil fuels.
Hover here Tropical deforestation alone is responsible for emitting 1.5 billion tons of CO₂ annually, equivalent to the emissions of 400 million cars
Hover here Cumulative emissions from land use changes since 1850 are estimated to be around 190 GtCO₂, compared to over 1,000 GtCO₂ from fossil fuels
Hover here Between 2000 and 2020, the world lost nearly 100 million hectares of forest due to agricultural expansion, urbanization, and logging

Data-preparation activities

Plots are based on Per capita CO₂ emissions dataset (countries_data.csv).
To combine it with continents and population additionally we used another dataset World Population Dataset.
To obtain total emissions we multipied the population and emissions per capita.
Then we saved it to separate file with data for each year (inforamtion about country, continent, total annual emissions, emissions per capita and population). To get the boudaries of each country we used world.geo.json file generated from geojson-maps with the lowest resolution (clear visualization purposes).

Additionally we needed to modify names of some countries that they will refer to the ones from our dataset. There were also no data for population of some countries in all given years (Cote d'Ivoire, Bonaire Sint Eustatius and Saba, Saint Helena, Kosovo and some of them didn't have data for emissions Puerto Rico, N. Did you know that Africa is actually larger than North America?

Most people are used to the Mercator projection, but the Equal Earth projection shows the true size of countries more accurately. This makes it better for showing data such as CO₂ emissions, where the actual size of a country is important for understanding the data.

The Mercator projection is the one most people are familiar with because it's often used in maps and applications like Google Maps. But you should know that it distorts the size of countries, especially near the poles! For example, Greenland and North America look much larger than they really are compared to Africa.

You can see these differences by looking at projections placed side by side!
